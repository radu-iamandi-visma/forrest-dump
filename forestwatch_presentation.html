The Problem

The data already exists. Nobody is reading it.

The Hansen Global Forest Change dataset has tracked tree-cover loss at 30 m resolution every year since 2000. It's free, global, and open — but interpreting "is this clearing illegal?" still takes a remote-sensing analyst. ForestWatch puts that judgment one click away.

The Agent

Orchestrator + one specialist sub-agent.

🧭 deforestation_agent
Sub-agent specialized in remote-sensing analysis. Takes a GeoJSON polygon, calls its own tools, and returns a structured verdict (severity, likely-illegal flag, confidence, summary).
📐 analyze_deforestation
Queries Hansen tree-cover-loss data for the polygon over the past 10 years. Reads from a local GeoTIFF when available; falls back to the GFW Data API. Returns per-year hectares lost.
📷 get_area_satellite_thumbnail
Builds a Google Static Maps satellite URL with the polygon outlined in red, embedded as markdown in the response so the LLM can show the user where they pointed.
Verdict Patterns

What the sub-agent flags.

🚨

Severe Loss

> 50 ha total or sustained heavy loss across multiple years

⚠️

Significant Loss

1–50 ha total, or a clear spike concentrated in one or two years

📉

Spike Year

Sudden jump after years of stability — classic illegal-clearing signature

📊

Gradual Pattern

Steady low-level loss — usually managed harvest, not necessarily illegal

🟢

Minor / No Loss

< 1 ha — noise from the 30 m grid, or genuinely intact canopy

🧭

Likely-Illegal Flag

Heuristic: spikes in remote / protected-looking areas raise concern, gradual loss in managed forest does not
